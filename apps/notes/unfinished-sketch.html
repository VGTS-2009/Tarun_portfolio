MAY 14, 2025 / MAKING

In praise of the unfinished sketch

A rough drawing leaves room for the next person to imagine what it could become.

STARTING BEFORE YOU KNOW

I used to think a first draft was supposed to prove that I had already solved the problem. That made every blank page feel like a test, and every early idea feel too small to show anyone.

LEAVE A LITTLE SPACE

Then I started sharing work while it was still a little lopsided. A box drawn in a notebook, a clumsy clickable prototype, a sentence that was nearly right. People could see where the idea was going and, more importantly, where it might go instead.

The unfinished sketch is generous. It says: this is a beginning, not a verdict. You can bring your own experience to it. You can ask a better question than the one I started with.

THE NEXT DRAFT

Now I try to make the first version clear enough to react to, but loose enough to change. The polish can come later. At the beginning, it is more useful to make something that invites an honest response than something that pretends to be final.
